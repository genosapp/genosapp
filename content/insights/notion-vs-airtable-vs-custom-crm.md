Every founder starts the same way: a Notion database, then an Airtable base, then — at some point — the question of whether to build something custom. Here's how to actually decide, instead of guessing.

## Notion: good for early, single-operator tracking

Notion CRMs are free, fast to set up, and fine when one person owns the pipeline. The moment you need real automation — auto-assigning leads, triggering emails on status change, syncing with a booking tool — Notion starts fighting you. It's a database with a nice UI, not a workflow engine.

## Airtable: the right tool for small teams with light automation

Airtable is the honest middle ground. It handles multi-user pipelines, basic automations, and integrations reasonably well, and it scales further than most people expect. The problems show up at scale: per-seat pricing gets expensive fast, complex automations hit real limits, and you're always working inside someone else's data model.

## Custom CRM: worth it when one of these is true

**1. Your workflow doesn't fit a generic pipeline.** If your sales or client process has steps no off-the-shelf tool models cleanly (multi-stage approvals, industry-specific statuses, unusual data relationships), you're bending your business to fit the software instead of the other way around.

**2. Per-seat cost is punishing growth.** Airtable and similar tools charge per user. Past a certain team size, a flat-cost custom system is simply cheaper — and you own the data outright.

**3. You need it wired into your actual stack.** A custom CRM talks directly to your booking tool, your invoicing, your product — no Zapier duct tape, no sync delays.

**4. You want it to be an asset, not a subscription.** A custom CRM is software you own. Cancel a SaaS plan and you lose your pipeline history; own the system and you never do.
