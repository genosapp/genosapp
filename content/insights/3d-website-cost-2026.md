Every week someone asks us the same question: *"What does a 3D website actually cost?"* Most agencies dodge it with "it depends." Here's a straight answer.

## The short version

A production-grade **interactive 3D website** — real-time WebGL, custom motion, mobile-safe fallbacks — typically runs **$5,000–$12,000** for a landing experience. At GenOS our 3D Hero Sites start at **$5,000** with a fixed scope and a 2–4 week timeline.

That's not a template with a spinning cube dropped in. It's a bespoke [interactive 3D website](/services/3d-websites) built with React Three Fiber, custom GLSL shaders, and performance engineering so it runs at 60fps and still works on a phone.

## What drives the price

Three things move the number:

1. **Custom shader work.** Off-the-shelf effects are cheap. A signature look — dispersion glass, a bespoke particle system, a shader nobody else has — takes real engineering time.
2. **Scroll choreography.** A static 3D scene is one thing. A scene that reacts to scroll, cursor, and section transitions is a motion-design project on top of the 3D.
3. **Performance guardrails.** The hard part isn't making it look good on your machine — it's making it fast everywhere. DPR clamping, fallback rendering, and reduced-motion handling are what separate a demo from a product.

## When 3D is worth it (and when it isn't)

3D is worth it when **being remembered is the point** — a product launch, a premium brand, a portfolio that has to out-class competitors. It is *not* worth it for a page whose only job is to rank for a keyword and convert on price. Use the right tool for the job.
