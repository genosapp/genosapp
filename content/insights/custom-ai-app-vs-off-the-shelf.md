The AI tool market is flooded. For almost any workflow there's a SaaS product that does *something* close to what you need. So when is it worth building a [custom AI app](/services/ai-apps) instead of just buying one?

## Buy when the tool fits your workflow

If an off-the-shelf tool matches how you actually work, buy it. You get instant deployment, someone else's maintenance burden, and a predictable subscription. Building custom software to replicate a $30/month tool is almost never rational.

## Build when one of these is true

**1. Your data can't leave.** If the value depends on your private, proprietary, or regulated data, a generic tool is a liability. A custom app keeps the data in your control. Our own product, [Aura](/work/aura), was built precisely on this principle — a voice AI that stores **zero** audio files by design.

**2. The workflow is your edge.** If the way you do the thing *is* your competitive advantage, bending it to fit someone else's software throws away the advantage. Custom software encodes your process.

**3. Per-seat pricing is eating you alive.** SaaS that charges per user punishes you for growing. At a certain team size, a flat-cost custom tool is simply cheaper — and you own it.

**4. You need it to actually integrate.** Off-the-shelf tools integrate with what *they* choose to. A custom app integrates with your exact stack, in your exact way.

## The honest middle path

You don't have to pick a side. The smart move is often: buy the commodity layers, build the one differentiated feature that only you need. Our [AI App Sprint](/services/ai-apps) is designed exactly for this — one production AI feature, shipped in 2–4 weeks, wired into what you already run.

## The real question

It isn't "build or buy." It's *"where is AI actually a differentiator for us, versus a convenience?"* Spend custom-build budget on the differentiators. Buy the conveniences.
